# The Question Way: the framework

Source: three short videos by the creator Ross (one with guest Reckless Ben); see
`creator-video-notes.md`. Their numbers and algorithm claims are unverified; the structure is
what to test.

## Core law

Interest holds while a question the viewer cares about is open. **When a loop closes and no
other loop is open, viewers start to leave.** Hollywood calls the closures plot points. The
hook is only the first loop ("level zero"); retention is decided at every closure after it.

## Tool 1 — The two-question hook

Open two loops, answer the first one immediately.

> "The two best ways to keep viewers watching are, number one, opening loops… but people
> always do this wrong."

In a few seconds the viewer gets value (way #1), a held question (way #2), and a new one
("what do they do wrong?"). The fast payoff earns trust; the open loops buy time.

**Failure it fixes:** one question, answered two minutes later with something obvious
("communication"). The viewer feels cheated by the time it lands.

## Tool 2 — Twist the obvious answer

If the viewer already understands the answer, don't explain it. Add a turn that makes the
explanation a new question: "but it's not what you think," "but this is where everyone gets it
wrong," "that's what everyone says, and it's half the story."

## Tool 3 — Problem → solution → bigger problem ("the Rick and Morty method")

State the problem in the intro and solve it fast ("glad I clicked"). Then show that the
solution creates a bigger problem. Solve that, and expose the next. The viewer keeps getting the
reward of progress but never the feeling of being finished, which is the moment they leave.

## Tool 4 — Stakes

Stakes are the second retention engine next to questions. They don't have to be dramatic: a
deadline, a cost, something that could go wrong. The source creator says stakes can be made up;
that only holds for obvious fiction. For anything presented as real (tests, results, reviews,
claims), stakes must be true.

## Tool 5 — Overlap the loops

Open the next loop **before** the current one closes, or in the same sentence that closes it.
Never put a full stop on every question at once until the end.

## Tool 6 — Show the method inside the video

"Notice how I just did that." Demonstrating the technique while teaching it is proof, and it
rewards attentive viewers.

## Tool 7 — The ending

Two honest options:

- **Close and convert:** answer the last loop and put the CTA in the same second, before the
  drop. (Ross even names the drop: "your interest declines right about now.")
- **Hand off a loop:** leave one question open and say where it is answered (next episode,
  long version). Say it out loud; a silent unanswered loop is bait.

## Diagnosis with the retention graph

Every cliff on the retention graph should line up with a loop that closed with nothing behind
it. Write down which question closed at each drop, then fix the next version by opening a new
loop before that point (measurement: the platforms' native analytics).

## What's style, not method

The source videos are raw phone selfies with no captions, music, or b-roll, plus a crude,
cocky personality. That shows the structure works without production, not that production
should be dropped. Keep the brand's own voice and edit; add the loop structure on top.
